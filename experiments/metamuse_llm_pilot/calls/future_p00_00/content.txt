The parent rule `-(remaining - item)` is equivalent to `item - remaining`. It strongly prefers the tightest fit (smallest `remaining`), which is the classic Best Fit heuristic. However, Best Fit can waste small amounts of space that are too tiny for future items, leading to fragmentation.

We can improve this by introducing a nonlinear penalty for bins that are nearly full but leave a small, likely unusable gap. We calculate the gap `g = remaining - item`. A gap that is a larger fraction of the remaining space is less risky. We penalize tiny gaps using the ratio `g / remaining`, which is small for bad tiny gaps and large for safe proportional gaps. We also slightly scale the item preference to avoid completely overriding Best Fit's core rationale.

```python
def score(remaining, item, capacity, index, n_bins):
    g = remaining - item
    return item * 1.05 - remaining + g / remaining
```
