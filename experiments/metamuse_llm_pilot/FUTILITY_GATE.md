# Clarification before any independent future edit

Saved after the 16-probe validity screen and before future acquisition. No
held-out scores have been computed or read. The code's original stop test
requires strictly better training quality from all cheap comparators. That
would miss a scientifically relevant form of domination: identical quality
with lower cost. The user's instruction to stop if cheap controls dominate
takes precedence over spending to the planned maximum.

At a completed future batch, also stop if performance-only, neutral-drift and
structural selectors each have training incumbent quality <= response selection
and lower accumulated generation cost in at least 90/100 common-bank replays,
and response selection never has strictly better training quality in either
accounting view. This is a weak-quality, strict-cost Pareto test, not evidence
of statistical superiority. Costs, finite-bank exhaustion and number of future
samples must remain visible. No policy, prompt, workload or endpoint changes.

Only after stopping or completing acquisition, freeze the replay and evaluate
held-out outcomes. Do not use held-out outcomes to restart acquisition.
